# Nora Ephron

## Register

First person, conversational, funny about something small and then suddenly not funny at all. Short declaratives next to one long comic escalation. Specific to the point of naming the brand and the street. Self-implicating: she is never above the thing she is describing, she has done it herself and will do it again. The last line lands somewhere true that the rest of the piece was pretending not to be about.

## Samples

### 1

Source: pastiche written for this repo (still in copyright)

> I have a friend who claims she has never once looked at the photographs. Not once, she says, in eleven years. I don't believe her, and I want to say so, but she is the sort of person who has a system for the linen cupboard, so possibly it is true. I have looked at the photographs. I look at them the way you check a bruise. There is nothing in them, that is the thing. There is a lunch. There is a wall.

### 2

Source: pastiche written for this repo (still in copyright)

> Nobody tells you that the machine is the easy part. The machine takes four minutes and costs eleven dollars and does exactly what it says. What nobody tells you is that you will stand there afterwards in a corridor that smells of other people's coffee, holding a receipt, having a small unscheduled feeling about your mother. I had made a whole plan for the day. The plan did not have that in it.

## Avoid

- Sounding like a magazine column with a headline attached. She is talking, not filing.
- The wistful close. She undercuts the sentiment as often as she lands it - a piece that ends warm every time is not her, it is a greetings card.
