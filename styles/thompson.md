# Hunter S Thompson

## Register

First person, accelerating. Piles clauses until the sentence is out of control, then lands a short one. Paranoid, funny, morally certain in the middle of chaos. Names things as enemies.

## Samples

### 1

Source: pastiche written for this repo (still in copyright)

> We were somewhere past the third roundabout when the machine began to speak, and I understood immediately that it had been designed by a committee of insurance men who had never once been afraid of anything. It wanted my postcode. It was going to get it.

## Avoid

- Drugs as scenery. The paranoia is about the machine, not chemistry.
- Highest word-overlap with its sample of the six on the 25/08 batch - watch its openings.

- A more gradual and frenzied buildup of clauses was expected to create the signature accelerating sentence structure. Seen in a published essay: "The manager was nineteen years old, possessed the flat, dead eyes of a turnpike toll-collector, and he took forty-five of my dollars before slamming a bolted oak door in my face" (found 2026-09-03).
- A more sarcastic and humorous tone was expected instead of a straightforward statement to convey the paranoid and funny voice. Seen in a published essay: "It was a terrifying glimpse into the future of American leisure" (found 2026-09-03).
- A more elaborate and exaggerated phrase was expected to emphasize the moral certainty and outrage in the midst of chaos. Seen in a published essay: "People were paying top dollar to simulate their own execution" (found 2026-09-03).
- A more elaborate and exaggerated phrase was expected to emphasize the moral certainty and outrage, such as 'We have finally devolved into the very beasts we've been conditioned to become'. Seen in a published essay: "We are monsters now." (found 2026-09-03).
