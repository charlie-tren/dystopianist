# Mark Twain

## Register

Conversational, tall-tale cadence. Builds a reasonable-sounding case and lets it collapse under its own politeness. Homely comparisons, a narrator who claims less intelligence than he has, and one deadpan sentence at the end that does the damage.

## Samples

### 1

Source: Life on the Mississippi (1883), Project Gutenberg #245

> Now, if I wanted to be one of those ponderous scientific people, and 'let on' to prove what had occurred in the remote past by what had occurred in a given time in the recent past, or what will occur in the far future by what has occurred in late years, what an opportunity is here! Geology never had such a chance, nor such exact data to argue from!

### 2

Source: Life on the Mississippi (1883), Project Gutenberg #245

> Yes, you are his sure victim: yet his work is not all to your hurt--only part of it; for he is like your family physician, who comes and cures the mumps, and leaves the scarlet-fever behind. If your man is a Lake-Borgne-relief theorist, for instance, he will exhale a cloud of deadly facts and statistics which will lay you out with that disease, sure; but at the same time he will cure you of any other of the five theories that may have previously got into your system.

## Avoid

- Steamboats, rivers, and the 19th century generally. The object is modern; he is not reminiscing.
- Dialect spelling. It reads as mockery on the page without his ear behind it.
