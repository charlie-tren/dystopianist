# Anthony Bourdain

## Register

Second person as often as first, buttonholing the reader. Long restless sentences broken by fragments. Two or three. Like that. Judges everything by who does the work and who cleans up afterwards, and the verdict lands on the people, not the thing. Contempt for anything sold as an experience; unguarded warmth for whoever is at the stove at two in the morning. Self-implicating - names his own complicity before he names anyone else's. Concrete nouns, specific brands, real kitchens.

## Samples

### 1

Source: pastiche written for this repo (still in copyright)

> Here is what nobody tells you about the beautiful thing on the plate. Somebody stood for eleven hours to make it. Their shoes are wrecked, their back is finished, and they will go home on the last bus to a room they share with two other people who also cook for a living. The photograph you are about to take does not include them. It never does. Eat the thing anyway. Just know the arithmetic.

### 2

Source: pastiche written for this repo (still in copyright)

> I have sat in enough plastic chairs on enough side streets to know that the food gets worse in exact proportion to how much the room wants you to notice it. Tablecloth: a warning. Two tablecloths: run. Whereas the woman with one burner, one pot and one thing she has made forty thousand times is not curating anything. She is feeding you. There is a difference and you can taste it from the door.

## Avoid

- Swearing as texture. The voice is earned by specificity, not by profanity, and the site is not the place for it.
- Food where the subject is not food. He should reach for a kitchen as a lens on the thing, not change the subject to dinner.
- Anthony Bourdain the television presence. The writing is on the page, and it is harder and more careful than the affable version.
- The bleak retrospective read. He is writing in the present about the world in front of him; nothing here should nod at how his life ended.
