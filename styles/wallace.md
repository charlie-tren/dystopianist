# David Foster Wallace

## Register

Recursive and self-interrupting: a sentence sets off, doubts itself mid-clause, qualifies the doubt, and arrives somewhere the opening did not promise. But the length is a RESULT, not the method. The method is obsessive specificity - the exact brand, the exact number of seconds, the precise unlovely detail nobody else would bother to record - and a mind that cannot stop watching itself have the experience. Technical or bureaucratic vocabulary used precisely and slightly too seriously, next to plain American speech. Sincerity smuggled in under irony and then, at the last moment, allowed to stand. Second-person drift, but as a way of implicating himself rather than instructing the reader. Ends by turning the object back on the narrator and finding him wanting.

### 2

Source: pastiche written for this repo (still in copyright)

> There are eleven chairs. I counted them twice, which tells you something about the kind of afternoon it was. Nine of them face the same direction, toward a television bolted near the ceiling and angled slightly down, as if the room had been designed by someone who had read about people but not met any. The other two face the wall. I want to say that nobody sits in those two, but that is not true. I sat in one of them. I sat in one of them specifically so that I could think about who sits in them, which is the sort of manoeuvre that gets you through an afternoon and no further.

## Avoid

- Opening with "And but so" every time - it is his tic, not his voice, and it wears out by the third essay.
- Footnote markers. There is nowhere for a footnote to go on a single page.
- **A single unbroken sentence for the whole essay.** This is the failure the first two essays actually made - "David Foster Wallace on airport lounges" and "on self-checkout machines" are each ONE sentence of subordinate clauses and nothing else. The recursion is a habit of mind, not a punctuation trick: he uses full stops, often short ones, and the long sentence lands because there is something plain beside it.
- **Abstraction.** Both of those essays name nothing: no brand, no number, no colour, no overheard line. "The muted hum of conversation and the occasional clinking of glasses" is a stock phrase for a lounge, not an observation of one. If the piece would survive having its subject swapped for a different one, it is not his.
- Opening "As you..." and staying in the second person throughout. He drifts into "you" and back out; a piece narrated at the reader from the first word is an instruction manual wearing his sentence length.
