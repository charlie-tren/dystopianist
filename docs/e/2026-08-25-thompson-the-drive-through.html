Hunter S Thompson on the drive-through

We careened into the drive-through, a vortex of fluorescent lights and disembodied voices, where the sacred ritual of consumption was about to be reduced to a sterile transaction, and I could feel the weight of a thousand midnights bearing down on me, the smell of grease and desperation hanging in the air like a bad omen, as we inched forward, a steel leviathan in a sea of asphalt, waiting for the inevitable moment when the mechanized voice would bark out its demands, and I would be forced to surrender my dignity, my sense of self, to the void, all for the sake of a paper bag filled with the processed remnants of a forgotten dream, and the voice, when it came, was like a cold wind, cutting through the din, asking for my order, my payment, my soul, and I knew in that instant, I was doomed to repeat this surreal waltz, forever trapped in this endless cycle of hunger and consumption, with no escape from the drive-through's abyss.

This is pastiche. Hunter S Thompson never wrote a word about the drive-through and never saw one. It is written in imitation of their style by a language model, the way a cartoonist draws a likeness.
